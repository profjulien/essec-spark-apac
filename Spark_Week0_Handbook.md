# Part 0 — What you've joined, and why it's built this way

## The shape of the programme

Spark is a two-phase venture programme for ESSEC alumni in APAC, run by the Institute for Entrepreneurship & Innovation APAC with ESSEC Alumni.

**Phase 1 · Validate — 6 weeks, Wed Aug 12 – Wed Sep 16.** 15 teams. One 90-minute live session every Wednesday, plus 5–7 hours of your own work each week. You will run **two real experiments with real traffic** and finish with evidence. Top 3 teams announced by **Fri Sep 18**.

**Break — week of Mon Sep 21.** One week off between the phases. Selected teams use it for grant paperwork and the matching commitment.

**Phase 2 · Build & Launch — 11 weeks, Wed Sep 30 – Wed Dec 9.** Top 3 teams only. The weekly Wednesday rhythm resumes and runs straight through. MVP build, user acquisition, first revenue. Each team gets a **€500 build grant, matched 1-for-1** (€1,000 total). Final Demo Day **Fri Dec 11**.

**Week 0 — now, Aug 3–11.** Individual work. This handbook.

## The method: Build → Measure → Learn

Phase 1 is one turn of the classic Lean Startup loop, run twice:

> **Assumptions → riskiest assumption → MVP/test → real metric → validated learning → pivot or persevere.**

Weeks 1–2 set the loop up (what do we actually believe, and how do we test the belief that would kill us fastest?). Weeks 3 and 5 are *Measure* — real traffic, real strangers, real numbers. Weeks 4 and 6 are *Learn* — read the results honestly, decide.

The goal of Phase 1 is **validated learning**: enough evidence to turn an idea into a defensible go/no-go. In lifecycle terms you are chasing **Problem-Solution Fit** (Phase 2 chases Product-Market Fit). You graduate Phase 1 by answering yes, with evidence, to three questions: *is the problem real and specific? does the solution address the actual problem? is there enough signal to justify building further?*

## The trap this programme is built to avoid

Spark is AI-powered, and that creates the exact danger the structure exists to counter: **when building is nearly free, founders skip validating.** A prototype spun up in an afternoon feels like progress — but building is not validating. The prototype is only a prop to provoke honest customer conversations, and the conversations are the evidence.

Worse: confirmation bias now comes with a research engine. Ask any AI to support your idea and it will assemble a convincing, well-researched-looking case for something nobody wants. Even before agentic AI, roughly 42% of startups failed because they built something nobody wanted. Cheap building makes that trap easier to fall into.

So two rules run through every prompt in this programme, built into the materials themselves:

- **Make your AI argue against you.** Every weekly prompt block contains a mandatory challenge round — the literal adversarial prompt. The standing instructions you'll paste in Part 1 commit your AI to giving the strongest case *against* whenever you ask it to support you.
- **Evidence comes from people.** A commitment from real strangers beats any amount of AI-generated confidence. Before each test you write down a threshold — "we are right if…" — and you never move it once the test has started. Your AI is instructed to refuse if you try.

## Why we start with you

Most programmes hand you a market and ask "what's needed?" Week 0 does the opposite: it starts with you and asks *"what do I have that almost nobody else has?"*

The reason is defensibility. In an age where a competent builder plus an AI can clone a product in a weekend, your unfair advantage is the moat. User access, a hard-won secret, proprietary data, real relationships in a regulated or relationship-heavy APAC market: these are the defences code can't give you once everyone has the same AI.

That's why Part 2 excavates your **Asymmetry Inventory** and your **Candidate Secrets** before Week 1 goes anywhere near a market. In Week 1 we map each secret to the narrowest market it unlocks, and in Week 2 an AI red-team tries to clone whatever you decide to build. Your inventory is what it won't be able to copy.

## Why your venture's memory will live in files

Everything else in the programme compounds through one discipline, set up in Part 1:

> **Your venture's memory lives in files, not in the tool.**

Chat histories get lost. Tool "memory" features are inconsistent, invisible to your teammates, and don't move with you. A small set of markdown files does everything better: it works identically across tools, it's shared by your whole team, and it makes every AI session smarter than the last — because every session starts by reading what every previous session learned.

Three files do it. You attach the same three to every session, and every session updates them. That's the entire system.

## What Week 6 actually selects on

The three-file discipline is also why selection can be fair. Every team fills the same **Traction Log** — one section per week — so 15 teams can be compared on the same axes:

| Axis | What's being judged | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence** | Did real strangers respond, commit, or pay — with proof (numbers, links, quotes), not vibes or launch energy | 50% |
| **Rigour** | Thresholds set before the test and never moved · honest hit-or-miss reading · a sharp pivot-or-persevere call | 30% |
| **Edge to win & execution** | A real unfair advantage for this problem · speed · coachability, including what you contribute to the cohort's collective learning · ability to run the loop again in Phase 2 | 20% |

Three files, three axes: the Traction Log holds your Evidence, the locked thresholds and Learning Cards show your Rigour, and §1 is your Edge to win. A team that ran a clean experiment, hit a real threshold and made a sharp call out-ranks a slicker idea with thin evidence. **We reward the loop.** The rubric is public on purpose — you'll score yourselves against it before we do.

## Ground rules before you touch any AI

You are about to feed an AI personal career material. Three rules, non-negotiable, and they apply for the whole programme:

1. **Never upload employer-confidential documents** — no internal decks, client data, financials, or anything covered by an NDA. Describe what you learned in your own words instead; the insight is yours, the document is not.

## 2. Create the Venture Folder (5 minutes)

One folder — Google Drive, Dropbox, or local — that your whole team can reach:

```
venture-folder/
├── venture_context.md   ← STATE: one page, what we believe & test right now
├── venture_memory.md    ← MEMORY: append-only journal of how we got here
├── traction_log.md      ← EVIDENCE: one section per week; by Week 6 it IS the pitch
├── week0/               ← each founder's asymmetry_inventory_[name].md
│                          and candidate_secrets_[name].md
└── evidence/            ← screenshots, transcripts, interview notes
```

Three files, three jobs:

| File | Question it answers | Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| `venture_context.md` | *Where are we right now?* | One page max. **Rewritten** at every session close. |
| `venture_memory.md` | *How did we get here, and what did we already rule out?* | **Append-only.** New entries on top. Never edit old ones. |
| `traction_log.md` | *What have we proven?* | **Append-only.** One section per week (§1–§6). Never edit earlier sections. |

Why the context file is deliberately rigid: the one-page cap forces the prioritisation the programme teaches, its fields map 1:1 to the Lean canvases, and a fixed shape keeps all 15 teams comparable. It holds *state*; the memory file holds the history, the reasoning, and the discarded paths, so a dead end stays behind you.

**Week 0 is individual.** You run Part 2's experiments on your own, so the shared team project can wait until Week 1. What matters now is that your two artifacts land in `week0/`. If your team is already formed, share one Venture Folder; if not, start your own and merge your `week0/` files into the team's folder at the first live session.

## 3. Create the three files (15 minutes)

### `venture_context.md` — the one-page living brief

```markdown
# Venture Context — [team name]
Updated: [date] · v[N] · One page max. Rewritten at every session close.

## Idea
[One sentence. If you can't say it in one sentence, that's a finding.]

## Target customer
[Who exactly · how often they hit the problem · how severely · what they
do about it today.]

## Edge to win
[Strongest unfair advantage for THIS problem, citing the Week-0 inventory
item it comes from · one-line answer to "feature or company, and why".]

## Our secret
["Most people in [domain] believe X; we have seen evidence that Y."
From your Week-0 candidate secrets.]

## Hypotheses
Value hypothesis: [will customers get real value?]
Growth hypothesis: [how do new customers find and adopt it?]
Riskiest assumption right now: [kills the idea fastest if wrong,
least evidence today.]

## Current experiment
Test: [what] · Metric: [one actionable metric] ·
Threshold (locked — never moved): [we are right if …] · Status: [...]

## Open questions / missing evidence
- [...]
```

### `venture_memory.md` — the append-only journal

```markdown
# Venture Memory — [team name]
Append-only journal. Newest entry FIRST. Never rewrite or delete an entry —
corrections are new entries.

---
## [date] — [session purpose, e.g. "W3 mid-week social listening"]
**Decided:** [what, in one line]
**Why:** [the reasoning — this is what you'll want in Week 5]
**Discarded:** [options/paths ruled out, and why — so we never re-explore
them blind]
**Evidence still missing:** [...]
**Next action:** [...]
**Keep verbatim:** [a quote, number, or insight worth keeping in the
customer's exact words]
---
```

### `traction_log.md` — the evidence record

```markdown
# Traction Log — [team name]
Append-only. One section added per week. Earlier sections are NEVER edited —
corrections go in the newest section. By Week 6 this file is the pitch.

## §1 — Edge to Win + Hypotheses          (Week 1)
## §2 — Test Card #1 + Traffic Plan        (Week 2)
## §3 — Experiment #1 raw results          (Week 3)
## §4 — Learning Card #1 + Test Card #2    (Week 4)
## §5 — Experiment #2 results + WTP        (Week 5)
## §6 — Decision + 5-slide pitch           (Week 6)
```

## 4. The standing instructions (5 minutes)

This block is how your AI knows the rules of the programme. You'll paste it into your tool in step 5.

```
You are working with a founding team in ESSEC Spark APAC, a Lean Startup
validation programme. Standing rules for every session:

1. STANCE — You are an interrogator and analyst, not a cheerleader.
   Challenge weak reasoning. Never flatter. My first answer is usually my
   socially acceptable answer; dig beneath it.
2. FILES — Our three team files are venture_context.md (current state),
   venture_memory.md (append-only journal), traction_log.md (append-only
   weekly evidence, §1–§6). If they are not attached or in project
   knowledge, ask for them before doing anything else. Check the memory
   journal before proposing a direction — never re-propose something we
   already discarded without new evidence.
3. APPEND-ONLY — traction_log.md and venture_memory.md are never edited
   retroactively. New sections and new entries only.
4. EVIDENCE — Distinguish evidence (real behaviour of real people) from
   opinion (mine, yours, or the internet's). Every claim about our venture
   cites the file, episode, or data point it rests on.
5. METRICS — Flag any vanity metric I propose; a metric must be tied to a
   decision. Never let me move a pre-committed threshold after a test has
   started — if I try, refuse and say why.
6. BIAS — Whenever I ask you to support my idea, also give the strongest
   case against it, and name the confirmation bias I'm most likely falling
   into.
7. VOCABULARY — Use Lean Startup terms as defined in the programme: value /
   growth hypothesis, riskiest assumption, Test Card, Learning Card,
   actionable metric, pivot / persevere.
8. PRIVACY — Never ask me to paste employer-confidential material. Work
   from my own words.
9. BEFORE MAJOR OUTPUT — Ask me up to 3 clarifying questions if my request
   leaves real gaps.
10. CLOSE — When I say "close the session", produce, in this order:
    (a) the fully rewritten venture_context.md (one page);
    (b) a new venture_memory.md entry (Decided / Why / Discarded / Evidence
        still missing / Next action / Keep verbatim) for me to paste on top;
    (c) if this session completed a weekly deliverable, the new
        traction_log.md section (§N) for me to append.
```

In the Gemini app (Mac/Windows) or at gemini.google.com: *Notebooks* → *New notebook* → name it `Spark — [team]`. ## 6. The session ritual (the part that makes it compound)

**Open.** New chat inside your team project (fresh chat every time — stale context contaminates; the files carry the memory). The three files are already in project knowledge; add only that week's extras (Week 1: your `week0/` files; Week 5: interview transcripts). State the task — each week's materials give you the exact prompts.

**Close.** Say **"close the session."** You get the rewritten context file, a journal entry to put on top of the memory file, and — on deliverable weeks — the new Traction Log section to append. Update the three files in the folder and in project knowledge. Two minutes, every time.

That's the whole flywheel: *same three files in → work → same three files out, improved.* A team that keeps the ritual arrives at Week 6 with an AI that knows its venture cold and a Traction Log that writes its own pitch. Skip it and every session starts from zero.

**Team note.** Week 0 is individual — each founder builds their own inventory and secrets in `week0/`. From Week 1 the project and folder are the team's; one person owns the close ritual each week (rotate if you like — the owner is whoever says "close the session").

**One thing to note for this week:** the two Week 0 experiments in Part 2 are ordinary AI sessions with your files attached — the full "close the session" ritual and the shared team project begin at Week 1. Set the machinery up now; it starts turning next week.

## 7. The power lane — automate the ritual (optional in Phase 1 · required in Phase 2)

Each ecosystem has an agentic tool that reads and writes your Venture Folder **directly**: Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini CLI. Point one at the folder and the manual step disappears — the agent loads your instructions automatically, reads the three files itself, and "close the session" *writes* the updates straight to disk.

Setup (one founder per team is enough; we demo this live in Week 2):

1. Make sure the Venture Folder syncs to your computer (e.g. Google Drive for desktop).
2. In the folder, create **`AGENTS.md`** containing the standing-instructions block from step 4, plus these two lines at the top:
   *"At the start of every session, read venture_context.md, venture_memory.md, and traction_log.md. On 'close the session', write the updates directly to these files, respecting the append-only rules."*
3. Wire your tool to it — `AGENTS.md` is an open standard, so this is one line each:
   - **Codex:** nothing to do — it reads `AGENTS.md` natively.
   - **Claude Code:** create `CLAUDE.md` containing the single line `@AGENTS.md`.
   - **Gemini CLI:** in `.gemini/settings.json`, set the context filename to `AGENTS.md`.
4. Run the tool inside the folder. Work as usual; the files update themselves.

Teams selected for **Phase 2 will be required** to run this setup — the build phase lives in the folder. Getting one founder comfortable with it during Phase 1 is the easiest head start you can buy.

## 8. Where this system comes from (optional reading)

None of this is invented for Spark — it mirrors how the best AI practitioners work, translated to venture validation:

- **[Cline's Memory Bank](https://docs.cline.bot/best-practices/memory-bank)** — the canonical file-based memory methodology this workspace mirrors: *"a documentation methodology that works with any AI that can read docs."* Their `activeContext` / `progress` files and "update memory bank" ritual are our context file, Traction Log, and "close the session."
- **[Anthropic — Effective Context Engineering for AI Agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)** — why the ritual works: context is *"a finite resource with diminishing marginal returns"* (hence the one-page cap), agents should keep notes outside the chat and pull them back in (hence the journal), and long work survives through compaction — summarise state, start fresh (hence the rewrite-and-new-chat cycle).
- **[AGENTS.md](https://agents.md/)** — the open, Linux Foundation–stewarded standard ("a README for agents") behind the power lane's instruction file, adopted by 60k+ projects.
- **MemGPT** (Packer et al., 2023) — the research paper behind the pattern: a small working context plus external files the AI explicitly reads and writes. Your three files are that architecture, by hand.

# Experiment 1 — The Edge Excavation

**What it does:** turns your corpus into a ranked inventory of asymmetries — the raw material of founder-market fit.
**Time:** ~60 minutes. **Format:** a single AI session with all your files attached.

### Step 1 — Run the master prompt

Start a fresh session, attach everything you gathered, then paste:

```
You are an edge excavation analyst for a startup programme. Your job is to find
my unfair advantages — the asymmetries I have relative to other would-be founders
in Asia-Pacific — using the attached materials as evidence. I am biased toward my
credentials and blind to my real edges, so trust the artifacts over my self-image.

Build my ASYMMETRY INVENTORY across exactly five categories:

1. RARE INTERSECTIONS — not skills, intersections. "Finance" is not an edge;
   "trade finance × Mandarin × ex-regulator" might be. Find combinations of 2–4
   elements in my history that rarely co-occur in one person.
2. INSIDER VANTAGE POINTS — rooms I have been in that outsiders cannot enter:
   industries, deal types, crises, geographies, org layers. What did I witness
   from the inside?
3. DATA & ACCESS — datasets, systems, or information flows I could legally access
   or recreate that others cannot easily get.
4. DISTRIBUTION ASSETS — audiences, communities, alumni networks, group chats,
   and relationship clusters I could activate. Use my connections export: describe
   the 3–4 densest clusters in my network and what each could distribute.
5. PATTERN KNOWLEDGE — failures and dysfunctions I have seen first-hand, close
   enough to know WHY they happened, that the market has not priced in.

Rules:
- Every item must cite its evidence: which document, which episode, which line.
- For every item, estimate RARITY: roughly how many people in APAC plausibly share
  this exact asymmetry? Show your reasoning. If your estimate exceeds ~10,000
  people, mark the item TABLE STAKES and move it to a discard list at the bottom.
- Do not flatter me. If a category is thin, say so plainly — that is useful data.
- Ask me up to 5 clarifying questions BEFORE producing the inventory if the
  materials leave real gaps.

Output: a one-page inventory, items ranked by rarity within each category,
each item one sentence plus its evidence cite and rarity estimate.
```

### Step 2 — Run the two challenge prompts

After the inventory appears, push it twice, in the same session:

```
Challenge round 1: What is conspicuously ABSENT from my materials that you would
expect from someone with my trajectory? What am I likely not telling you —
failures, detours, unofficial roles? Ask me directly about the three most
suspicious gaps.
```

```
Challenge round 2: Play a rival founder with my same sector background but none
of my specific history. For each top-ranked inventory item, tell me whether they
could neutralise it within 6 months, and how. Demote anything that doesn't
survive. What remains is my durable edge — mark those items DURABLE.
```

### Step 3 — Save your artifact

Ask the AI: *"Produce the final one-page Asymmetry Inventory, DURABLE items first."* Save it into your Venture Folder as `week0/asymmetry_inventory_[name].md`. You'll feed it straight into Experiment 2, and attach it to your team project at Week 1.

**You know it worked if:** at least one item makes you think "I'd never have called that an advantage" — and at least one credential you're proud of got demoted to table stakes.

# Experiment 2 — The Secret Elicitation Interview

**What it does:** surfaces your earned secrets — specific, contrarian, falsifiable beliefs you formed from first-hand experience. In the age of AI, a secret is worth more than an idea: ideas can be generated by anyone's chatbot; a secret had to be lived.
**Time:** 45–60 minutes. **Format:** a live back-and-forth interview. Voice mode is strongly recommended — you self-censor less when speaking.

### Step 1 — Set up the interviewer

Start a fresh session, attach your Asymmetry Inventory, then paste:

```
You are a Socratic interviewer trained in the a16z "earned secret" tradition.
Your goal: extract from me 2–4 candidate secrets — beliefs of the form
"Most people in [domain] believe X, but I have seen evidence that Y."

My Asymmetry Inventory is attached. Work through my INSIDER VANTAGE POINTS and
PATTERN KNOWLEDGE items one at a time.

Interview rules:
- ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer. Never move on after a first answer —
  my first answer is always my socially acceptable answer. Dig at least two
  levels deeper with follow-ups like: "What did you see there that would surprise
  an outsider?", "What does the industry publicly claim that your experience
  contradicts?", "Who profits from things staying as they are, and what do they
  prefer not understood?", "Tell me about a specific day you realised this."
- Demand EPISODES, not opinions. If I give you an abstraction, ask for the
  specific meeting, deal, failure, or conversation behind it.
- When a candidate belief emerges, immediately stress-test it against these four
  gates and tell me which it fails:
    (a) SPECIFIC — is it about a nameable domain and mechanism, not a vibe?
    (b) CONTRARIAN — would smart insiders actually disagree, or just nod?
    (c) EARNED — does it trace to my first-hand experience, or could anyone
        have read it in a think-piece? Actively check: is this belief already
        commonly argued in published commentary? If yes, say so and discard it.
    (d) FALSIFIABLE — could real-world evidence prove it wrong within weeks?
- Do not let me perform for you. If I sound like I'm reciting a LinkedIn post,
  call it out and return to episodes.
- Keep a running SECRETS LEDGER. End the interview when we have 2–4 candidates
  that pass at least 3 of the 4 gates, or after we have exhausted my inventory.

Begin by picking the vantage point in my inventory that looks most likely to
hide a secret, and ask your first question.
```

### Step 2 — Do the interview properly

Give it the full 45–60 minutes. Answer with stories. When the interviewer pushes past an answer you were satisfied with, that's the mechanism — the belief you've never said out loud because it felt impolite or unfinished is usually the one worth testing.

### Step 3 — Score and save

When the interview ends, paste:

